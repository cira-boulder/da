Real-time RRFS-Smoke Hybrid Data Assimilation of VIIRS/GOES-16/17 AOD and Surface PM2.5 Observations for Improved Regional Smoke and Fire Weather Forecasting

Funding AgencyNOAA Project NumberNA23OAR4590111 Project PIsHuiying Luo, Blake Allen, Liaofan Lin, Anton Kliewer Performance Period2022–2027

Description:

Wildfire smoke can spread far from its source, and local smoke concentrations can change quickly as winds and other weather conditions evolve. This project advances the Rapid Refresh Forecast System’s smoke data assimilation capability from GSI in RRFSv1 to JEDI in RRFSv2. The team combines aerosol optical depth (AOD) observations from VIIRS and GOES satellites with surface PM2.5 measurements to better constrain smoke in the forecast model. This work complements ongoing development of wildfire emissions and smoke transport in RRFS. The project also includes real-time monitoring and verification to support an eventual transition to NOAA operations and improve forecasts of smoke, air quality, and visibility.

CIRA Research of/for Bipartisan Infrastructure Law: Improving Approaches to Extreme Precipitation Estimation for Infrastructure Hazard Risk Planning

Funding AgencyNOAA Project NumberNA25OARX405C0034-T1-01 Project PILiaofan Lin Performance Period2025–2027

Description:

This project supports NOAA’s effort to modernize probable maximum precipitation (PMP) estimates used in dam safety and high-hazard infrastructure planning. CIRA and its partners are developing and testing physics-based weather model configurations for simulating historical extreme precipitation events. The team improves model initialization through data assimilation, compares simulated storms with historical observations, and develops machine-learning tools to correct systematic precipitation errors. These comparisons help identify which configurations best reproduce observed storms and which errors need postprocessing. The work advances the National Academies’ recommendation to combine weather model simulations and statistical correction in updated PMP estimation methods.

Disaster Relief Supplemental Appropriations: Assimilation of Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) Smoke and Dust Products in the Rapid Refresh Forecast System

Funding AgencyNOAA Project NumberNA23OAR4050134D Project PIsHuiying Luo, Liaofan Lin, Anton Kliewer Performance Period2023–2026

Description:

Wildfire emissions depend on the surrounding weather, and the smoke and particles they release can in turn affect local weather. Under FIRE 3, CIRA researchers are developing data assimilation methods to improve the initial distribution of smoke and dust in the Rapid Refresh Forecast System. The project uses satellite aerosol optical depth products for both smoke and dust, along with near-surface PM2.5 measurements. A better representation of these particles at the start of a forecast helps the model account for their interactions with radiation and cloud microphysics. The goal is to improve forecasts of temperature, winds, and moisture in and around areas affected by wildfires.

CIRA Research of/for Bipartisan Infrastructure Law: Refining Numerical Approaches to Extreme Precipitation Estimation for Infrastructure Hazard Risk Planning

Funding AgencyNOAA Project NumbersNA23OAR4050419I
NA24OARX405C0012-T1-01 Project PILiaofan Lin Performance Period2023–2026

Description:

Many existing estimates of probable maximum precipitation (PMP) rely on historical storms with limited observations and methods that assume a stable climate. This project assessed how NOAA’s operational forecast and analysis products could support updated PMP estimates and tested numerical weather prediction methods for simulating extreme storms, with consideration of how a changing climate could affect those estimates. GSL’s contribution focused on improving data assimilation, exploring ensemble postprocessing, and evaluating different model dynamical core configurations. This work helped establish which datasets and modeling methods could support more reliable extreme precipitation estimates for dam safety and water resources management.
